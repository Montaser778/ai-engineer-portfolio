The problem, in business terms

A voice or agent feature is blocked, slipping the roadmap, or shipped in a state that doesn't survive real usage — real network conditions, concurrent users, or a language the prototype was never tested in. Each week of delay has a cost, whether that's a missed launch window or continued manual work the feature was meant to remove.

Recommendation

A fixed-scope engagement de-risks the decision: the price is fixed before work starts, unstarted milestones are fully refundable, and the code belongs to the company on completion. It is the lowest-commitment way to validate the approach before considering a larger hire.

Step three

Objection cheat-sheet

"Why not use our own team?"

Because they're already committed elsewhere, or this isn't their specialism — real-time voice and multi-agent systems are a narrow skill set most product teams don't carry in-house. If your team has the bandwidth and the specific experience, you shouldn't hire him — that's the honest answer.

"Why not an agency?"

Agencies typically cost more and add account-management overhead, and you rarely know in advance which individual engineer will actually do the work. A direct specialist contract skips both, at the cost of one point of failure instead of a team — which is exactly what the risk-reversal terms are designed to offset.

"What if he becomes unavailable mid-project?"

Milestones are scoped so each one delivers working, demoable software — nothing is held back until a single final delivery. If something happened mid-milestone, you'd be holding working code and clear documentation up to that point, not a folder of unfinished work. This is a real trade-off of working with an individual rather than a firm, worth weighing honestly against the cost difference.

"How do we even pay someone abroad?"

Standard international wire transfer or a freelance-platform contract (e.g. Upwork), both offered — whichever fits your procurement process. This is raised and settled on the scoping call before any commitment.
