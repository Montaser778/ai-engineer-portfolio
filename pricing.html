FAQ

Questions about the money

The main variables are integration surface (how many existing systems the work has to talk to), whether real-time audio or low-latency requirements are involved, and how well-defined the scope is going into the scoping call. A greenfield feature with a clear spec estimates tighter than a retrofit into an unfamiliar codebase. Ambiguity is the single biggest cost driver — most of the scoping call is spent removing it before a number is quoted.

Small clarifications inside the original intent are absorbed at no charge — that's normal for any real project. A genuine scope change (a new feature, a different integration, a materially larger surface) is quoted as a separate milestone with its own price and timeline, agreed before work starts on it. The original milestones are never renegotiated after the fact.

The fixed price holds. Estimating is his job, not the client's risk — if a milestone runs long on the build side, that's absorbed rather than re-billed. This is also why scope is defined tightly before a price is quoted: a fixed price only works when both sides agree on what's actually being built.

Hourly billing puts the overrun risk on the client and rewards slower work, even unintentionally. Fixed scope means the incentives point the same direction: finishing well and on time is what gets paid. Hourly advisory is still offered separately, but only for review and audit work where there is no fixed deliverable to scope against.

Third-party costs — model inference, hosting, telephony minutes, paid APIs — are billed directly to the client's own accounts, not marked up or bundled into the quote. Ongoing infrastructure operation after the support window ends is covered under the retainer, not the original project price. Anything outside the agreed milestone scope is quoted separately, as above.

The same terms apply regardless of history: 50% upfront, 50% on delivery, and any unstarted milestone is fully refundable. For a first engagement, the Sprint tier or a single Build milestone works well as a low-commitment way to establish trust before a larger scope of work is agreed.
